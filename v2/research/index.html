The instrument

Labels were the ceiling.

Aristotle named ethos, pathos, logos and kairos. Two thousand years later, Robert Cialdini named six principles of influence. A label tells you a lever is present; it cannot say how hard it presses, on whom, or when. Fields stall at the label stage until an instrument arrives.

  1. SignalThe message, in order: text by position, audio by time, video by frame.
  2. Rhetorical MassHow much was said: claims, entities, structure, fallacies. The same text always gives the same mass.
  3. Persuasive VelocityHow well it was said, across eight channels, down to leaves that each carry a score and its reasons.
  4. Momentum and forceWhat moved. Force is what it takes to change the course of a conversation.
  5. The personaWho is listening: who they are, what moves them, their state of mind, their setting.
  6. Persuasive ResonanceDid it land? Four probabilities, one person, one message, one moment.

Persuasive Velocity

Eight channels.

How well something was said, read on eight channels, each down to leaves that carry a score and their reasons.

EthosDo I believe the source?
PathosDoes it reach me through feeling?
LogosDo the steps connect?
KairosIs this the right moment, at every scale?
StyleHow vividly is it put?
ClarityDoes it feel easy to grasp?
FocusDoes it capture and hold attention?
AwarenessDo I see the move as a move? The channel Pulp cares about most.

Persuasive Resonance

Did it land?

At the top of the instrument a message meets a person, and the readout reports four probabilities that always sum to one.

An illustration, not a result. Every number in a real readout carries its stated reasons, and every score is computed against a goal someone stated.
