Enter the timeline

Technology doesn't make you a better thinker. It never has. Habits do.

Every new tool changes what people practice, and AI is no different.

AI doesn't care about people. People do, and they're who we build for: people who want to argue better, listen longer, and change their minds for the right reasons.

Walking away solves nothing. We'd rather do the work than sit it out: make it clearer what people actually said, what they meant by it, and what the machine did with it.

Why people decide

One argument splits four ways: who says it, how it feels, its logic and its timing. Numbers for machines, plain words for people.

The instrument

Aristotle named the forces. We measure them.

Six rungs, from the words on the page to whether they landed with one particular person.

  1. SignalThe message, in order: text by position, audio by time, video by frame.
  2. Rhetorical MassHow much was said: claims, entities, structure, fallacies. The same text always gives the same mass.
  3. Persuasive VelocityHow well it was said, across eight channels, down to leaves that each carry a score and its reasons.
  4. Momentum and forceWhat moved. Force is what it takes to change the course of a conversation.
  5. The personaWho is listening: who they are, what moves them, their state of mind, their setting.
  6. Persuasive ResonanceDid it land? Four probabilities, one person, one message, one moment.
